Data and privacy

Information the Bot processes

The Bot processes information supplied by Discord and information needed to provide its enabled features. Depending on how a server configures the Bot, this may include:

  • Discord user, server, channel, role, and application IDs.
  • Usernames, display names, and avatar URLs when needed for moderation messages, embeds, or logs.
  • Server configuration, including enabled modules, exemptions, warning thresholds, logging channels, Word Filter rules, and other moderation settings.
  • Moderation records, including warnings, cases, actions, reasons, target IDs, moderator IDs, timestamps, and warning totals.
  • Appeal answers that a member submits through the appeal form, stored with the related case.
  • Scheduled message content and times set by administrators, and lockdown records (previous channel permissions) kept so a lockdown can be lifted.
  • Mod notes that staff write about a member, and server backups (role and channel names, permissions, and which members had which roles).
  • Case evidence that staff attach (links, and files re-posted in a staff channel), and the Roblox account IDs of banned members, kept so the ban evasion check can recognize them.
  • Invite records: which invite each member joined with, who made it, and where an invite link was posted in a channel the bot can read.
  • Anti-nuke records: who triggered it, what they did, and what the bot did about it.
  • Dashboard sign-in attempts: time, result, IP address, and browser, kept for the most recent 300 attempts.
  • Limited message content used by Automod. A recorded violation may include an excerpt of the triggering message, limited to 500 characters.
  • Recent message content may also be temporarily retained for spam and duplicate-message detection.
  • Information required for normal Discord bot operation, such as permissions, guild membership, and interaction data.

Some of this information exists only in memory while the Bot is running. The Bot does not use a built-in profanity dictionary. Word Filter rules are created by server administrators, and each rule may specify its own action.

When information is collected

Information is processed when:

  • A server administrator configures or uses a Bot feature.
  • A message triggers an enabled Automod, spam, duplicate-message, link, mention, caps, invite, or Word Filter rule.
  • The Bot records a moderation action, warning, threshold action, or server configuration.
  • Discord sends information necessary for the Bot to respond to commands, buttons, menus, or other interactions.

The Bot does not intentionally collect the contents of every message as a general chat archive. Message excerpts are retained only where needed for enabled moderation and detection features or a resulting moderation record.

Why information is used

Information is used to:

  • Detect and respond to configured moderation violations.
  • Delete messages and apply configured warnings, timeouts, kicks, bans, or temporary bans.
  • Maintain warning totals, thresholds, moderation history, and server settings.
  • Send moderation notifications and logs to channels selected by server administrators.
  • Diagnose errors, maintain reliability, and protect the Bot from abuse.

The Bot does not sell personal information or use moderation data for advertising.

Storage and service providers

Persistent data is stored in a file on the server that runs the Bot, operated by the Bot owner or its hosting provider. This file can be erased when the hosting service restarts or redeploys the Bot. Discord also processes information under Discord's own terms and privacy practices because the Bot uses Discord's API.

Data may be processed in the countries where the Bot, its hosting provider, or other service providers operate.

Retention

Server configuration and moderation records are retained while they are needed to provide the Bot and its history features. Automod tracking can retain warning totals, violation history, timestamps, IDs, and limited message excerpts. Recent-message data used for spam and duplicate detection is intended to cover only recent activity and is periodically trimmed.

The Bot does not currently promise a fixed deletion period for every moderation record. Server administrators may remove the Bot, clear supported settings, or request deletion or clarification from the Bot owner.

Access and control

Server administrators control which modules are enabled, which channels receive logs, and which moderation rules are configured. Moderators with access to the relevant Discord channels or Bot commands may see moderation information sent there.

The Bot owner and authorized operators may access stored data when necessary to operate, secure, troubleshoot, or maintain the Bot.
